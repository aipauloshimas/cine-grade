# Take design: the honest "before"

The before/after only lands if the BEFORE is a genuinely plain clip that
still contains the raw material the grade needs. Two rules pull in opposite
directions and both hold: **composition is free, light is honest.**

## The honest-before rules (real recording or AI generation)
- Natural light only: overcast daylight or window light. No lamps, no
  colored lights, no reflector fill.
- Neutral wardrobe: black, grey, cream, denim. One small warm accent (a
  beanie, a cap) is fine — it reads well under every grade. No statement
  colors, no costume, nothing that dates the frame to a decade or genre.
- Auto exposure, locked; no filter, no portrait mode (fake blur breaks the
  relight), no beauty mode.
- Camera propped, never handheld. Format: 16:9 whenever the take quotes a
  widescreen composition (the reveal depends on that geometry; crop to
  9:16 in the edit if needed); 9:16 only for close-ups that quote nothing.
- Plain environment: bare wall, empty lot, empty table. Nothing that
  denounces intention.

## Canvas: the big repaintable area decides the film
| Canvas | What to guarantee in the take | Films |
|---|---|---|
| **Open space / sky** | low horizon, deep perspective, subject small-to-medium, no buildings cutting the sky | Blade Runner 2049 amber, Pulp Fiction, Mad Max |
| **Side-lit shadow** | ONE window, subject ~60 cm from it at three-quarter, half the face in unfilled shadow — do NOT fill it | Moonlight, Se7en, Drive |
| **Close-up skin** | facing the window, face fills the frame, even soft frontal light | The Matrix, Her |
| **Sparse interior, high angle** | phone propped high in a corner looking down, plain table, bare walls | The Matrix (interrogation room) |

A blank canvas with nothing in it (sky only, no ground, no horizon) grades
well but is empty to watch. Give the frame geometry: perspective lines, a
horizon, a table edge, a window.

## Quote the film's framing (free, and it sells the reveal)
The raw take may borrow an iconic composition — the geometry is already the
film's before any color arrives, so the after is undeniable:
- Blade Runner 2049 Vegas: lone figure dead center, full body, monumental
  empty space, vanishing point behind, horizon low.
- The Matrix interrogation: raised three-quarter high angle down onto a
  seated subject at a plain table in a boxed-in room.
- Pulp Fiction trunk shot: camera lying low looking straight up, subject
  leaning over into frame against the sky.
- Moonlight: intimate close/medium, single soft source, face half in shadow.

## Motion without moving the camera
The camera is static and propped; the subject creates the reveal:
- walk toward the camera (wide → medium; silhouette → lit face)
- push up off a table and lean into a high lens
- lean in for a close hold, lean back to medium

## AI-generated take (Seedance 2.5)
Use the ugc-craft skill's anatomy (frame DNA, identity lock from the user's
own character reference, skin realism seal, diegetic camera, single-take
clauses). Dialogue engineering applies only when the take has lines; a
silent reveal take (walk-in, lean-in, weight shift) drops that block and
keeps everything else. Add these cine-grade clauses:
- Setting line ends with: *"muted neutral tones, completely ungraded flat
  automatic-exposure phone footage look"*.
- Light clauses per canvas (from the table above), e.g. side-lit: *"NO fill
  light, no second light source, a clear bright-to-dark gradient across the
  face"*.
- AVOID adds: *"no cinematic color grading, no color filters, no colored
  lights, no bokeh portrait blur"* plus the canvas bans ("no sunlight or
  blue sky" for overcast, "no objects on the wall" for sparse interiors).
- Wardrobe in the identity lock stays neutral per the honest-before rules.

Placeholders belong to the user: `[your character reference]`, their own
wardrobe, their own lines. This skill carries no character of its own.

## Series planning
One neutral wardrobe across all takes (coherent series), one canvas per
take, one film per canvas, three distinct moods across the set. Shoot in
sessions by light: an overcast exterior, a window interior, a sparse room.
